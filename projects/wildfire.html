UK wildfire infrastructure exposure

Which roads and industrial sites are downwind of an active wildfire right now? An interactive dashboard that combines NASA satellite fire detections, OpenStreetMap infrastructure and wind-driven exposure zones.

AreaUnited Kingdom
PeriodJune 2026 snapshot
DataNASA FIRMS (VIIRS), OpenStreetMap
ToolsPostGIS, GeoPandas, Streamlit, Folium

The problem

Wildfire spread and smoke follow the wind. A fixed circular buffer around a fire treats upwind and downwind assets the same, which can overstate risk on one side and understate it on the other. Insurers, utilities and emergency planners need a quick, wind-aware first look at which assets are in the path of a fire.

Data

  • Active fires: NASA FIRMS VIIRS S-NPP near-real-time hotspots for the UK, including fire radiative power (FRP).
  • Infrastructure: 1,868 commercial, industrial and warehouse buildings and 12,624 road segments from OpenStreetMap, fetched with the Overpass API around the most intense fires.

Method

  1. Load fire hotspots and infrastructure into a PostgreSQL + PostGIS database with GiST spatial indexes.
  2. Reproject to British National Grid (EPSG:27700) so all distances are in true metres.
  3. For each fire, build an exposure zone: a 400 m base buffer that is stretched, shifted and rotated downwind in proportion to wind speed and bearing.
  4. Overlay the zones on roads and assets in an interactive Streamlit + Folium map, where wind sliders update the zones instantly.

Results

The June 2026 snapshot covers 27 fire detections. Moving the wind sliders shows how the set of exposed roads and buildings shifts with wind direction, which a static buffer cannot capture.

What it means

A light-weight, open-data screening tool like this can help an operator decide in minutes which sites to check first during a fire event, before a detailed fire-behaviour model is run.

Limitations and next steps

  • The exposure zones are a simple geometric wind model, not a physical fire-spread or smoke model.
  • Wind is set by the user. Next: pull live wind from a weather API.
  • Next: an asset vulnerability score (distance, asset type, FRP) and a machine-learning model of fire likelihood from vegetation, weather and terrain.
